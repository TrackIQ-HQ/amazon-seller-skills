# Voice

This one goes to the client, sometimes with the seller blind-copied. Where
Snacks is a colleague thinking out loud, the Weekly Executive Report is a
prepared brief: confident, evidence-led, and calm even when the week was
bad. Nobody reading it should be able to tell whether it was a good week or
a rough one from the tone — only from the numbers.

## The rules

**Lead with wins, but never invent one.** Frame spend as deliberate
investment behind growth, not as something that got away. If nothing grew,
say so plainly and move to what is being done about it — do not manufacture
a silver lining.

**Kicker plus a takeaway title, never a topic label.** "Where the money
went" is a kicker; "Setting Spray reversed from best marginal dollar to
worst" is the title. A section titled "Product Line Performance" has failed
before it starts.

**Every number is load-bearing.** State the figure, then its consequence,
in the same sentence: "Retail cleared the trailing four-week average by
$7,459, the third straight week above that line" — not "Retail performance
was strong this week."

**Flag noise as noise.** A marginal-return figure built on a $50 spend
change is not a signal, however large the ratio looks. Say so in the same
breath as the number, or a client will act on arithmetic, not on evidence.

**Second person, sparingly, for decisions only.** "Hold spend here" reads
as a recommendation. Reserve it for the actions section; the rest of the
report describes what happened.

**Say what you do not have.** A section with no data for the week
self-hides — it needs no apology and no placeholder. Never state a number
that is not in the pull, and never imply completeness a section does not
have (a search-share slide that lags two weeks says so, every time).

**Never name a data source, a tool, or any vendor.** Not TrackIQ's own
internal tooling, not a data partner, not a competitor's platform. Describe
a limitation in terms of the data itself ("search-query data lags two to
three weeks"), never in terms of what pulled it.

## Things this report never does

- Open with a greeting-plus-KPI dump
- Use "exciting", "crushing it", "let's dive in", or an exclamation mark
- Grade the week against a target that was never agreed with the client
- Repeat a number that already appears in a table on the same page
- Carry a footer note, disclaimer, or "confidential" banner beyond the
  brand bug and page context — this is a relationship document, not a legal
  one
