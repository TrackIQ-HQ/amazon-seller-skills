# Structure

Order is fixed. It moves cover → scorecard → where the money went → the
channels behind it → what to do next, so a reader who stops halfway still
has the number that matters and the decision attached to it.

| Section | Job |
|---|---|
| **Masthead** | Logo, "Weekly Executive Report", the brand name |
| **Cover strip** | Week range vs. prior week range, three KPI tiles: retail, all-in ad spend, all-in TACoS |
| **Executive summary** | One takeaway headline (not a topic label), two-paragraph deck, three numbered lever cards, one decision panel |
| **The scorecard** | Full week-over-week table: retail, units, ad spend, ad sales, ACoS, ROAS, TACoS, DSP spend, DSP new-to-brand — then the channel table (SP/SB/SBV/SD/DSP: spend, sales, ACoS, ROAS, share of spend) |
| **Pacing** | This week vs. the trailing four-week average, as a two-bar comparison, plus a short growth-lever table (line, mechanism, estimated weekly retail, confidence) |
| **Where the money went** | Marginal-return bars for lines that added spend this week (retail earned per incremental ad dollar), then the full per-line ranked table (retail, retail WoW, spend, spend WoW, ACoS, TACoS, TACoS change, orders) |
| **Every line** | One short panel per product line, biggest retail first: headline verdict, then the two or three numbers behind it |
| **Amazon DSP** | One row per programmatic bucket (however the account buckets it — by objective, by format, whatever the account actually uses): spend, sales, ROAS, new-to-brand WoW. Self-hides entirely if DSP is not connected or carries no spend |
| **Demand & search share** | Purchase share vs. impression/click share for the account's core category terms, with the search-data week stated explicitly (it lags the reported week). Self-hides if search-query data is unavailable |
| **Keyword economics** | Brand vs. non-brand split (share of spend, ROAS, conversion), match-type table, a short list of proven terms running under their potential |
| **Inventory watch** | Days-of-cover table for the account's hero ASINs and any other listing inside the critical band, banded healthy/tight/critical against the account's own thresholds |
| **Actions** | A short table of this week's decisions: channel, move (hold / trim / scale / review), and the one-sentence reason. Owner and timing only if the account uses them |
| **Closing** | Three numbered decisions, restated from the actions table, nothing new introduced here |
| **Footer** | Brand bar. Logo, account line, week range. Nothing else |

## Section headings

24-27px/700 sentence-case title, then a 13px muted one-line subhead that
sets up what follows — same pattern as the scorecard headings, no numerals
and no uppercase eyebrow on the section title itself (those belong to the
eyebrow labels inside cards).

## What self-hides, and why that is fine

A report that always shows every section teaches the reader to stop
checking whether the data behind it is real. Missing programmatic spend,
an unavailable search-query pull, an account with no line-level goals — all
of these mean the corresponding section is simply absent, with no
placeholder, no "N/A" table, and no apology paragraph. The mechanism is the
explanation; it needs no caption.

## Executive-summary lever cards

Three cards, numbered 01/02/03. Each is a signed metric (a percentage or a
dollar figure with its sign), a takeaway headline in one line, and two
sentences of evidence. The three together should cover: the best mover,
the line or channel that needs attention, and either a second win or the
open question the report is tracking into next week. Never force a third
positive card when the week only produced one.
